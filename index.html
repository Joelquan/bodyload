    Early warning signs

      If you're already hurting

      Right now

      • Stop the motion causing the pain if you can — don't push through sharp or radiating pain.
      • Tell your supervisor or safety lead. Getting it on record matters, even if it feels minor.
      • Rest and gentle movement usually help more than staying fully still.

      See a doctor or PT if

      • Pain lasts more than a few days or keeps returning
      • You feel numbness, tingling, or weakness
      • Swelling, bruising, or reduced range of motion shows up

      This isn't a diagnosis — it's a nudge to get checked properly.

      Where this comes from

      Grounded in the official standard

      Bodyload's back-and-lifting risk is modeled on NIOSH's Revised Lifting Equation — the standard safety professionals actually use.

      NIOSH Revised Lifting Equation

      What it measures

      A Recommended Weight Limit (RWL) — the heaviest load nearly all healthy workers could lift over a full shift without raising their risk of back injury.

      The risk score

      Weight lifted ÷ RWL = Lifting Index. Under 1.0 is considered safe for most workers. Over 3.0 means high risk and calls for an immediate redesign of the task.

      What it doesn't cover

      • Only lifting — not standing fatigue, typing, driving, or hunched close work
      • Doesn't account for slippery floors, restricted space, or awkward environments
      • No official equivalent exists yet for most non-lifting jobs — that's the gap Bodyload fills

      Official tool

      NIOSH publishes a free calculator app, NLE Calc, used by safety professionals for lifting-specific assessments.

      Bodyload draws on published occupational health research for lifting tasks and applies the same underlying risk logic — frequency, duration, load, and body position — to postures NIOSH doesn't cover.
